1What goes in

Etter Tasks sheet

{{n_tasks}} tasks across {{n_roles}} roles. For each task: the role, its workload (a group of related tasks), its category (one of six) and the hours one person spends on it a month.

Why this sheet: every task has its own hours, category and description, so every number traces back to a named task. Etter's Workloads sheet splits the same hours differently across categories; still an open question for Etter.

Etter Roles sheet + client

For each role: headcount ({{headcount}} in total), average base salary and level, which maps to a turnover band.

Etter's own automation score for each role is compared with the task hours as a second opinion (flagged if more than 10 points apart), never mixed into the numbers.

Four choices, then the dials

The client picks a scenario, a policy for freed time (box 5), an industry, and whether to include assistant seats. Every other number sits on Inputs with a source and a grade: A official figure, B benchmark, C estimate, D thin evidence.

2Cost of an hour, kind of work

Loaded hourly rate

hourly rate = salary × {{oncost}} ÷ (12 × task hours a month)

1.31: of every $100 an employer spends on a full-time private-industry worker, $68.50 is wages and $8.10 paid leave (BLS ECEC, Table 5). A salary already pays for leave days, so 100 ÷ (68.5 + 8.1) = 1.31.

160 hours a month (1,920 a year), the Etter file's convention. Senior Analysts' tasks cover 136; shares are taken of the hours recorded.

Pay grows {{wage}} a year (BLS Employment Cost Index)NEW: salaries saved, capacity, severance and training time follow it. The cost test stays at today's prices.

Example, Analysts: {{a_sal}} × 1.31 = {{a_loaded}} a year ÷ 1,920 = {{a_rate}} an hour.

3What AI could do, what to build

Agent work (Directive, Feedback Loop)

AI-able hours = hours a month × headcount × most AI could do

Example, Analysts' Extract and format financial data: {{ex_h}} × {{a_n}} × {{ex_ceil}} = {{ex_able_m}} hours a month ({{ex_able_y}} a year).

Is the AI cheaper than the work?

One agent per workload ({{n_wl}} have agent tasks), shared by every role that does it, priced two ways:

Build: $55,000, or $130,000 if 2+ roles or 3+ agent tasks; $3.48 per AI hour; 18% a year upkeep. Buy: $15,000 / $35,000; $6 per AI hour.

value a month = AI-able hours × hourly rate
NPV at cost = (value × each role's net realisation − running − upkeep) × {{ann}} − up front

The cheaper option is kept; the workload passes if its NPV is above zero. {{ann}}: months of full value in five years after the 3-month build, the {{adopt}}-month ramp-up and {{disc}} discounting, at today's pay and prices.

Financial analysis and reporting: {{far_roles}} roles, {{far_value}} of work a month; build {{far_build}} vs buy {{far_buy}}, so {{far_opt}}. {{last_name}}: {{last_value}} a month; {{last_opt}}, {{last_npv}}, {{last_passes}}.

How big a programme

Passing workloads are ranked by return per dollar up front and added one at a time. The programme stops where five-year cash peaks: {{n_built}} of {{n_pass}} ({{n_buy}} bought, {{n_build}} built, {{upfront}} up front).

Cash counts only whole positions, so late, small workloads add cost but free fractions of people that never round up.

Go-live: pilot of the top 3 in month {{pilot_m}}, a 6-month evaluation, then waves of 10 in months {{waves}}. Gate, month {{gate_m}}NEW: scale only if the pilot reaches the {{be}} break-even; otherwise stop.

Assistant work (Task Iteration, Learning, Validation)

hours saved = hours × smaller of (most AI could do, band saving) × {{active}} active use

Seats roll out from month 2 over 6 months, {{lic}} a seat a year for people in post, trued down each contract yearNEW. Example, Analysts' Develop custom financial analyses: {{as_y}} × {{as_sav}} × {{active}} = {{as_out}} hours.

Stays human (Negligibility): no saving

4What AI really frees

AI takes over, people check it

hours freed = AI-able hours × takeover × (1 − review)

Takeover {{takeover}}: the share of AI-able work the agent actually ends up doing (range 20–62%, grade C). Review {{review}}: for every 100 hours it does, a Professional spends 30 checking (15–45%, C); by band {{rev_ladder}}NEW.

{{takeover}} × {{ex_keep}} = {{net}} net realisation, the one number the answer turns on. Running cost is paid on all hours the AI works, reviewed or not.

Example: {{ex_able_y}} × {{takeover}} = {{ex_ai_y}} AI hours; × {{ex_keep}} = {{ex_freed_y}} hours freed a year.

Ramped in, wave by wave

use in month m = S-curve from go-live, full after {{adopt}} months

The same curve drives the cost test, the running cost and the savings (MIT NANDA 2025: 6 to 24 months).

The work left behind is harder

freed × (1 − {{drag}} × share of the role's time freed)

Complexity drag, applied to agent and assistant time together. Grade D: a simple stand-in for the exception tail until Etter gives volumes or handling times.

Analysts free about {{ex_y}} of their time, so they keep {{ex_dr}}: {{ex_freed_y}} becomes {{ex_after}} hours, {{ex_people}} people's worth.

A removed position stays removed while the AI runs and never exceeds the time freed; if the programme stops, the work comes back and each is refilled at {{cph}}NEW. A role needs roughly 20–30 people before agents free a whole position.

Example, Analysts: {{ar_auto}} freed by agents × {{harvest}} = {{ar_harv}}, so {{ar_target}}. {{ar_leave}} Analysts leave in five years: "leave {{ar_target}} of the next {{ar_leave}} leavers unreplaced". All roles: {{positions}} positions, {{pos_share}} of FP&A, by month {{pos_month}}.

Hires avoided: against the plan

avoided = smaller of (spare whole people, planned hires so far)

Only if the industry is growing: spare freed time fills planned hires before it becomes capacity. FP&A has 0% growth, so 0; the healthcare pack avoids {{health_hires}}.

Capacity: time back NEW

left over = freed − (positions + hires avoided) × hours
capacity = left over − {{block_h}} hours a month × people in post

At the hourly rate, growing with pay: {{cap_fte}} people's worth at month 60. Every month, positions + hires avoided + capacity + stranded = hours freed, to 0.00000 hours.

Stranded: too thin to use NEW

Every role frees only {{per_lo}}–{{per_hi}} hours a month per person still in post. The first {{block_h}} (about {{block_min}} minutes a day) is slack nobody can redeploy: {{str_fte}} people's worth, valued at zero.

BFive fixes from the finance review

1 Capacity. Positions were whole people, but every leftover sliver counted as capacity at full cost. Now freed time below {{block_h}} hours a month per person is stranded at zero value. Capacity {{o_cap}} → {{capacity}}; cash unchanged.

2 Training. It was paid once, for the day-one headcount. Now {{joiners}} joiners are trained as they arrive, and everyone in post gets a {{refresh}} refresher each year. {{s_join}} joiners, {{s_ref}} refresher.

3 Licences. Seats were paid for all {{headcount}} people forever. Now they follow people in post and are trued down at each contract anniversary. {{s_lic}}.

4 Payback. It was undiscounted next to a discounted NPV. Now both are shown and labelled. Month {{pay_d}} discounted, {{pay_u}} undiscounted.

5 Stopping. The model could not show a programme that was tried and stopped. Now it can: AI off, the work comes back, positions are refilled at {{cph}} each. A gate at month {{gate_m}} stops every future that misses the break-even. {{p_stop}} of futures stop, {{stop_cost}} each. Gartner forecast at least 30% of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept.

CTwo parameters added

Wage growth, {{wage}} a year (BLS Employment Cost Index, sourced). Salaries saved, capacity, severance and training time grow with pay; the cost test stays at today's prices. {{s_wage}}. Range 2.5–4.5%: {{wg_lo}} to {{wg_hi}} ({{wg_rank}} of {{n_sens}} in the tornado).

Review rises with seniority, {{slope}} a band (judgment; direction from Brynjolfsson v2 and Dell'Acqua 2026). Review is {{rev_ladder}} from Para-professional to Executive. {{s_slope}}; programme {{wl_wage}} → {{n_built}} workloads, positions {{o_pos}} → {{positions}}. Range 5–40%: {{sl_lo}} to {{sl_hi}} ({{sl_rank}} of {{n_sens}}).

Both pass the working agreement's test for a new parameter: a top-ten mover in the tornado. At zero, each gives back the model before it exactly.

Not changed

The six steps; the Etter data; ceilings 90% / 80%; the three policies; Go at 70%, pilot first at 40–70%; the top three movers (harvest share, takeover, review); cash, hires avoided and capacity never added together.
